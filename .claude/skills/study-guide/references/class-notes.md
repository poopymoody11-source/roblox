## Teacher feedback to remember
- On imagery analysis ("Rip," p. 33): **"Magical hues is not enough. It just describes color. You need the preposition."** When analyzing imagery, quote the **full phrase** (the description plus what it's doing or where), not just one adjective. Follow this in every guide's setting/mood quotes.
- Allusion MOT: "For what reason might an author use an allusion? What is the point if the reader doesn't know the original source?" **Allusion = reference to an outside text.** Guides should always explain *why* an allusion matters, even if the reader doesn't know the source.

## Emerson homework (connects to every Unit 1 text)
The user's reading of Emerson's **Materialist vs. Idealist**:
- **Materialist** "takes departure from the external world." The user interprets this as writers putting their **surroundings into their characters**, so characters become a product of their surroundings. Example: **Rip** reflects Irving's hardships and his reliance on his family and brothers. Materialist writers also write about government and society.
- **Idealist** writers take thoughts and ideas and put them into writing. Example: **"Baker's Bluejay Yarn"** (the bluejays filling the house with acorns ≈ the **gold rush**).
- "Mind is the only place man can be perfect." Example: **"Young Goodman Brown"**, where seemingly good people are bad inside (the cult in the woods).
- The user was unsure about the second half of the handout. Clarify Materialist vs. Idealist simply when relevant: **Materialist = facts, the senses, the physical world. Idealist = ideas, spirit, imagination, consciousness.** Emerson considered Transcendentalists idealists.
