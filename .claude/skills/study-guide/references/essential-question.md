# Course essential question

**How can we critically interpret the American Dream?**  (Ms. McGuigan English)

## Units (from course map)

| Unit | Topic / EQ | Core texts |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Changing Structures in American Literature:** In what ways is literature a cultural artifact? Students examine the intersection of rhetoric, structures and themes in traditional, modern and postmodern American stories, building on previous short story, poetry, novel and graphic novel units. | Summer reading / goal setting. *Great American Short Stories*: "Rip Van Winkle", "The Fall of the House of Usher", "Baker's Bluejay Yarn", "Young Goodman Brown" |
| 2 | **The Puritans:** How have various writers viewed and critiqued America, a "city on a hill"? What were the Puritans interested in writing about, how were they seen by Americans of the 19th century, and how do we view them now? What is their legacy? | "Young Goodman Brown", "The Custom House", *The Scarlet Letter*, "Milk for Babes", "Peace, Love, and Puritanism" |
| 3 | **The Great American Novel:** In what ways does the fictional individual's quest for religious, political, and personal freedom reflect the struggles of individuals in American history? | *Adventures of Huckleberry Finn* |
| 4 | **American Odysseys:** myth of progress, the class system, desire to "light out for the territory." For what reasons do authors use satire to discuss the impact of politics and the American class system? | "Baker's Bluejay Yarn", *Huckleberry Finn*, "Hobo Travels" (McNamara's *Vagabond Travels Vol. 1*) |
| 5 | **Gilded & Jazz Age Stories:** How do fictional accounts of the Gilded and Jazz Age reflect the history of New York and America, and current cultural/political discourse? | *The Great Gatsby*, "The Theory of the Leisure Class", select nonfiction |
| 6 | **American Drama:** plausibility of the American Dream; plays as rhetorical constructs. | *Death of a Salesman*, *A Streetcar Named Desire*, poetry |
| 7 | **Modernism, Myth, and Point of View:** How does literature counter myths and realities? | *Fences* |
| 8 | **Changing Structures in American Literature** (review) | *The Things They Carried* |
